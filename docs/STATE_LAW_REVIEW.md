## Arkansas

- **Status:** Law identified

## California

- **Status:** Law identified
- **Citation:** Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.91.1 (SB 1027) (2014)
- **Summary:** Publishers of booking photographs may not solicit or accept payment to remove, correct or modify a photo, or to refrain from publishing it. The person pictured may recover the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation.
- **Open questions:** Separate, more recent laws reportedly limit when law enforcement may post booking photos on social media (Penal Code § 13665); confirm bill numbers and effective dates.
- **Sources:** <https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/13-14/bill/sen/sb_1001-1050/sb_1027_bill_20140815_chaptered.htm>
- **Reviewed by counsel:** No

## Colorado

## Florida

- **Status:** Law identified
- **Citation:** Fla. Stat. § 901.43 (2017)
- **Summary:** Anyone in the business of publishing arrest booking photographs may not solicit or accept a fee to remove them. After a written request to the publisher’s registered agent, the photo must be removed free of charge within 10 calendar days and not republished. Courts may order removal and impose a civil penalty of $1,000 per day for noncompliance.
- **Open questions:** A 2021 bill (HB 755) proposed widening coverage to ad-funded publishers; confirm whether enacted.
- **Sources:** <https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/901.43>
- **Reviewed by counsel:** No

## Illinois

- **Status:** Law identified
- **Citation:** 815 ILCS 505/2QQQ (2013)
- **Summary:** It is an unlawful practice under the Consumer Fraud Act for anyone publishing criminal record information, including booking photos, to solicit or accept a fee to remove, correct or modify it. Amended in 2019 (Public Act 101-0431) to add a duty to correct errors within five business days.
- **Sources:** <https://ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/081505050K2QQQ.htm>

## Louisiana

- **Status:** Law identified
- **Citation:** Act 281 of 2022 (SB 24) (codified section to be confirmed) (2022)
- **Summary:** Remove-for-pay websites must remove and destroy a booking photo within seven days of a request, without a fee, when the person was acquitted, not prosecuted, or had the charge expunged, vacated or pardoned and provides proof. Demanding payment can be prosecuted as extortion. Law enforcement may release booking photos only in listed circumstances (expanded by Act 303 of 2023).
- **Sources:** <https://www.ncsl.org/technology-and-communication/mug-shots-and-booking-photo-websites>, <https://legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1262041>
- **Reviewed by counsel:** No

## Oregon

- **Status:** Law identified
- **Citation:** ORS 646A.806 (2013)

Violations are unlawful trade practices.

## Utah
