Over the past decade, many states have passed laws aimed at websites that publish booking photos and then charge people to take them down. These laws vary widely, and many states have no mugshot-specific law at all. Knowing which type of law applies to you helps you choose the right next step.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Laws change, and how a law applies depends on the facts. Consult a licensed attorney in your state.

## The main approaches

### Bans on removal fees

Some states prohibit websites that publish booking photos from asking for or accepting payment to remove them. Violations may carry civil penalties, give the person pictured the right to sue, or be treated as a deceptive trade practice.

### Free removal on request

Some states require certain websites to remove a booking photo free of charge when the person pictured asks — often limited to people whose charges were dismissed, who were acquitted, or whose records were expunged or sealed — and set a deadline for doing so.

### Limits on where photos come from

Some states restrict law enforcement agencies from providing booking photos to sites that charge removal fees, or require requesters to promise how photos will be used.

### Limits on police publishing photos

A growing number of states restrict when police may publish booking photos themselves — for example, on social media — especially before a conviction.

## What this means for you

- **Never assume you have to pay.** If your state bans removal fees or requires free removal, you may not need to pay anyone to have a booking photo taken down.
- **Keep your paperwork.** Many protections apply only with proof of a dismissal, acquittal, expungement or sealing.
- **Remember the internet is national.** A website may be based in another state, and its own policies and search engine tools still matter.
