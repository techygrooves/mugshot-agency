If you have an arrest or conviction on your record, you have probably come across terms like expungement and sealing. They are often used interchangeably, but they are not the same — and every state uses its own terminology and rules.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** We are not a law firm. Whether you qualify for any form of relief is a legal question for a licensed attorney in your state.

## The common terms

- **Expungement** generally means a record is destroyed or treated as though it never existed, at least for most purposes. Some states call this expunction or erasure.
- **Sealing** generally means a record still exists but is hidden from the public and most background checks. Courts and some agencies may still see it.
- **Set-aside, vacating or annulment** generally refers to a conviction being cancelled or withdrawn, sometimes followed by sealing.
- **Nondisclosure or record restriction** — terms some states use for processes that limit who can see a record.

Our [state pages](/states/) note which term each state uses once our legal review of that state is complete.

## What relief can — and can't — do online

A court order changes official records. It does not automatically remove copies that websites have already published, and it does not bind every site on the internet. In practice, though, proof of expungement or sealing often helps a great deal:

- many mugshot and people-search sites will remove a record when shown a sealing or expungement order
- some states require certain websites to remove booking photos free of charge after a dismissal, acquittal or expungement
- search engines may drop pages that are removed or updated as a result

## A practical order of operations

1. Talk to a licensed attorney about whether you qualify for relief in your state.
2. If relief is granted, keep copies of the order.
3. Use it to support removal requests to each website that published your record — see [how to remove a mugshot from Google](/resources/how-to-remove-a-mugshot-from-google/).
4. Ask search engines to refresh results once pages are removed.
