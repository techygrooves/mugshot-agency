A booking photo that ranks for your name can surface in every background check, first date and job application. The good news is that there are several separate routes to getting it out of search results, and many of them cost nothing. Here is how the process works.

## 1. Find every copy

Mugshot websites copy records from each other, so a single arrest often appears on several sites. Before requesting anything, search for:

- your full name, with and without a middle name or initial
- your name plus the city or county where you were arrested
- your name plus words like "arrest", "booking" or "mugshot"
- the photo itself, using an image search

Keep a list of every URL you find. You will need the exact addresses for each request.

## 2. Ask each website to remove it

Many mugshot and news sites publish a removal or correction policy. Look for a "remove", "contact" or "opt out" link, and follow it exactly. If your case was dismissed, you were acquitted, or your record was expunged or sealed, include proof — it often makes the difference.

**Check your state's law before paying anyone.** Several states require certain websites to remove booking photos free of charge, at least when a case did not end in a conviction, and some make it unlawful for those sites to charge a removal fee at all. Our [state pages](/states/) explain what applies where once our legal review of each state is complete.

## 3. Ask Google to drop the result

Removing a page from a website does not instantly remove it from Google. Google offers several request routes:

### Sites that charge to remove content

Google has a policy that lets people request removal of pages about them from sites that require payment to take content down. Google has also said it uses these removals as a signal to rank such sites lower. Start from Google's help center and search for its policy on sites with exploitative removal practices.

### Pages that are gone or have changed

If a website has already deleted or updated the page but Google still shows the old version, use Google's [Refresh Outdated Content tool](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7041154). It asks Google to update or drop the stale result. It does not remove anything that is still live on the website.

### Personal contact information

If the page also shows your home address, phone number or email address, Google's "Results about you" tool can request removal of those results and alert you when new ones appear.

## 4. Watch for it coming back

Arrest records are public, and mugshot sites re-scrape them. Set up alerts for your name and check periodically, especially in the months after a removal.

## When the photo can't be removed

Some sites — such as news archives — may decline to remove accurate reporting. In that case, the practical option is **suppression**: building accurate, positive results for your name so the unwanted one moves down where fewer people see it. Read [removal vs. suppression](/resources/removal-vs-suppression/) for how that works.
