Data brokers are companies that collect personal information from many sources, combine it into profiles and sell or publish it. Some operate quietly in the background, selling data to marketers. Others — people-search sites — publish profiles anyone can look up by name.

## Where the data comes from

- **Public records** — property records, court records, voter files in some states, and professional licenses.
- **Commercial sources** — purchase histories, warranty registrations, subscriptions and loyalty programs.
- **Online activity** — information shared through apps, websites and social media.
- **Other brokers** — data is bought, sold and combined across companies.

The result can be a surprisingly detailed profile: current and past addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, age, relatives, and sometimes estimated income or interests.

## Why it matters

- **Safety.** A published home address makes it easy for anyone to find you.
- **Scams and fraud.** Detailed profiles help scammers impersonate you or target you convincingly.
- **Reputation.** People-search profiles can surface old addresses, associations or records in ways that are misleading or out of date.

## What you can do

- **Opt out.** Most major people-search sites offer an opt-out process. Our [opt-out guide](/resources/remove-personal-information-from-people-search-sites/) walks through it step by step.
- **Use your state privacy rights.** Some states give residents rights to request deletion of data held by brokers, and some require brokers to register with the state.
- **Ask search engines to help.** Google's "Results about you" tool can request removal of results showing your contact details.
- **Share less going forward.** Use separate email addresses for sign-ups and review app permissions.

## Why one round isn't enough

Brokers continually acquire fresh data, so removed profiles often reappear. Effective protection means repeating opt-outs on a schedule.
