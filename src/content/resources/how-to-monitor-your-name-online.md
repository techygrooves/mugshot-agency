Reputation problems are easiest to handle when they are small. A new review, forum post or article can climb into the top search results within days. Monitoring means you hear about it first — not from a client, employer or friend.

## Decide what to watch

Make a short list of the searches that matter:

- your full name, plus common variations and nicknames
- your name with your city, employer or profession
- your business name, products and key people
- any past issue you want to know about if it resurfaces

## Free tools worth setting up

- **Search alerts.** Search engines and news services let you create alerts that email you when new pages mention a phrase. Create one per search on your list, in quotation marks for exact matches.
- **"Results about you."** Google's tool can alert you when new search results show your home address, phone number or email address, and lets you request their removal.
- **Review platform notifications.** If you run a business, claim your profiles on the major review and map platforms and turn on notifications for new reviews.
- **Social media mentions.** Most platforms notify you when you are tagged; search periodically for untagged mentions too.

## Check manually, too

Alerts miss things. Once a month, search your list in a private browser window (so results aren't personalized) and note what appears on the first two pages. Keep a simple log so you can see what changed.

## When something new appears

1. **Don't react immediately.** Read it carefully and save a copy with the date and URL.
2. **Check whether it breaks a rule** — a platform policy, a site's terms, or a privacy protection.
3. **Decide on a response:** a reply, a removal request, a correction request, or simply strengthening positive results around it.
