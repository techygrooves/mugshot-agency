Search your name and there is a good chance a people-search site will show your current address, past addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, age and relatives — often for free. These sites, sometimes called data brokers, compile public records and commercial data into profiles anyone can look up.

## Why it matters

Exposed contact details make it easier for strangers to find you, for scammers to target you, and for someone with a grudge to show up at your door. For people in sensitive jobs, or anyone who has dealt with harassment, removing this information is a basic safety step.

## How to opt out

Most major people-search sites offer an opt-out process. It usually works like this:

1. **Find your listing.** Search the site for your name and the states you have lived in. There may be more than one profile.
2. **Locate the opt-out page.** It is often linked in small print in the footer, under "privacy", "do not sell" or "opt out".
3. **Submit the request.** You will typically provide the profile URL and an email address for confirmation.
4. **Verify.** Many sites send a confirmation link or ask for a phone verification. The request may not be processed until you complete it.
5. **Check it worked.** Search again after the stated processing time. Removal from the site does not instantly update search engines.

Use a separate email address for opt-outs if you can, and share no more information than each form requires.

## Removing the results from Google

Even after a site removes your profile, Google may show a cached copy for a while. Google's "Results about you" tool lets you request removal of results that show your home address, phone number or email address, and can alert you when new ones appear. If a page has already been deleted or changed, Google's [Refresh Outdated Content tool](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7041154) can update the stale result.

## Why profiles come back

Data brokers continually buy and compile new records. A profile you remove today can be rebuilt next month from a fresh data source, and new sites appear regularly. Effective privacy protection means repeating the process on a schedule, not doing it once.

## Your rights may vary by state

Some states have privacy laws that give residents additional rights over data held by brokers, such as the right to request deletion. What applies depends on where you live and the company involved.
