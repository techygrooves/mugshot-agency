A cluster of fake one-star reviews can drag down a rating that took years to build. The good news is that every major review platform prohibits fake and conflicted reviews. The catch is that only the platform can remove them, and only when they break its rules.

## Is it actually against the rules?

Platforms generally do not remove reviews simply because they are negative, harsh or unfair. They do act on reviews that break their content policies. Common grounds include:

- **Fake or incentivized reviews** — written by someone who was never a customer, or posted in exchange for payment.
- **Conflicts of interest** — reviews by competitors, former employees, or the business owner.
- **Off-topic content** — reviews about a different business, or rants about something unrelated to the customer experience.
- **Harassment, hate speech or threats.**
- **Personal information** — reviews that publish someone's private details.

Read the specific platform's review policy before reporting, and match your report to the rule that was broken.

## How to report effectively

1. **Use the platform's own reporting tool.** Most have a "report" or "flag" option next to each review, and a separate process for business owners.
2. **Pick the most accurate reason.** A precise reason gets better results than a general complaint.
3. **Add evidence when the form allows it.** For example: no record of the reviewer as a customer, a sudden burst of reviews from new accounts, or details that clearly describe another business.
4. **Stay factual.** Describe what is wrong with the review, not how upsetting it is.
5. **Keep a record.** Note the date, the review and the reason you gave, so you can follow up.

## While you wait

Reviews under evaluation can stay visible for some time, and some reports are declined. A short, professional public reply protects you in the meantime — see [how to respond to negative reviews](/resources/how-to-respond-to-negative-reviews/).

## What never to do

Do not answer fake reviews with fake positive ones. Fake reviews break platform rules, and federal rules on consumer reviews and testimonials prohibit them. They can make a bad situation much worse.
