A news story can follow someone for years — even when the case was dropped, the facts changed or the story was only half told. Because news sites are well established, their pages tend to rank highly and stay there.

## First, read it closely

Separate three things:

- **Factual errors** — wrong names, dates, charges or outcomes.
- **Outdated information** — accurate when published, but no longer the full picture (for example, charges later dismissed).
- **Opinion or accurate reporting you dislike** — usually the hardest to change.

What you can realistically ask for depends on which of these you are dealing with.

## Ask the publisher

Many news organizations have a corrections process, and some will consider updating or adding a note to older stories in specific situations — such as dismissed charges or sealed records. Some newer policies even consider removing names from minor, old stories.

- Write to the editor or corrections contact, not the reporter's social media.
- Be brief, polite and specific about what you are asking for and why.
- Include documentation, such as a dismissal or sealing order.
- Accept that publishers decide independently, and many decline.

If the publisher updates the article, ask search engines to refresh the result — Google's [Refresh Outdated Content tool](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7041154) handles pages that have changed.

## If it stays up: suppression

When an article won't change, the practical option is to make sure it isn't the first thing people see. Build and strengthen accurate pages that deserve to rank for your name — a professional website, complete profiles, published work and community involvement. Read [removal vs. suppression](/resources/removal-vs-suppression/) for how this works.
