When something damaging shows up for your name, there are two fundamentally different ways to deal with it. Knowing the difference helps you set realistic expectations — and spot anyone promising more than is possible.

## Removal: taking the content down

Removal means the content is deleted from the website that hosts it, or de-indexed so it no longer appears in search results. It is the cleanest outcome when it is available.

Removal is most often possible when:

- the website has a removal or correction policy that applies to you
- a state law gives you a right to removal — for example, some states' rules on mugshot websites
- the content violates the platform's own rules, such as a review that breaks a site's guidelines
- the content includes personal information that a search engine's policies protect, such as your home address or phone number
- the page has already been changed or deleted, and the search engine is simply showing an outdated copy

Removal is usually *not* available for accurate news reporting, court records or opinions that do not break any rule.

## Suppression: pushing it out of sight

Most people never look past the first page of results. Suppression works with that fact: instead of deleting the unwanted result, you build and strengthen accurate, relevant pages that deserve to rank above it.

Typical suppression assets include:

- a personal or business website
- complete, professional profiles on reputable platforms
- published articles, interviews or community involvement
- business listings and directories

Suppression takes longer than a successful removal request and needs ongoing upkeep, because search results keep changing. It also never deletes anything — the content still exists if someone digs for it.

## Why most plans use both

Real situations are rarely clean. A single arrest might appear on six mugshot sites (often removable), a local news story (often not) and a people-search profile (usually removable through an opt-out). The sensible plan removes what can be removed, then suppresses what remains.

## A warning about promises

Search engines and website owners make their own decisions, and rankings move. Be cautious with anyone who guarantees that specific content will disappear by a certain date, or who offers to post fake reviews or misleading content — those tactics can violate platform rules and the law, and can make things worse.
