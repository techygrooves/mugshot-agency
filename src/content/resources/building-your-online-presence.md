When someone searches your name and finds very little, a single negative or mistaken result can dominate the page. When they find a clear, professional picture of who you are, that one result matters far less. Building that picture is the foundation of personal reputation management.

## Start with what you control

- **A personal website.** A simple site at a domain with your name is one of the strongest assets you can have. Include a short bio, your professional background and a way to get in touch.
- **Professional profiles.** Complete profiles on the major professional networks and on industry or licensing directories relevant to your work.
- **Consistent details.** Use the same name, photo and short description across profiles so search engines connect them to you.

## Add substance

Profiles alone only go so far. Results that rank well tend to have real content behind them:

- articles or posts about your field
- talks, interviews or podcast appearances
- volunteer and community involvement
- professional achievements and certifications

Everything should be accurate. Exaggeration is easy to spot and undermines the trust you are trying to build.

## Keep it current

An abandoned profile with a five-year-old job title doesn't help. Set a reminder to review and update your main profiles a few times a year.

## Check your privacy at the same time

While you're building a public presence, review what you don't want public: old social media posts, tagged photos, and people-search listings that show your address.
